### Changed

- **The `entry_point` census now reads every tracked `.py`, not only those under `hooks/`, and accepts a loop over the console module's pairs as an answer (#1255).** An entry point here is a tracked `.py` file with a module-level `if __name__ == "__main__":` block. Its answer is the set of streams it puts into UTF-8 before it does its work, and `hooks/lib/console.py#STREAMS` holds the one answer every entry point owes: `stdin` and `stdout` with `replace`, and `stderr` with `backslashreplace`. A script that prints a character its console cannot encode dies with its stdout empty, and an empty stdout is how a hook says it found nothing. The census in `tests/censuses.py` reads the first module-level `__main__` block of each file, and no later one. It accepts two forms of the answer as the block's first statement: the call `console.to_utf8()`, or a `for` loop over a literal tuple of pairs equal to `STREAMS` whose body calls `.reconfigure(encoding="utf-8", errors=<errors>)` on the stream it binds from `getattr(sys, <name>, None)`. It reads `STREAMS` out of `hooks/lib/console.py` and keeps no copy of it. It refuses a block among whose own statements it reads no answer at the block's `if` line, an answer that is not the block's first statement at that first statement, and a loop it does not read as `STREAMS`' pairs in that form at its `for` line. The refusals at the `if` line and at the `for` line say what the census did not read, not that the script gives no answer: a call nested under another statement, or a loop over `console.STREAMS`, gives the answer and is refused, because the census reads neither spelling. The refusal at the block's first statement names the line of the answer the census read there. The refusals at the `if` line and at the `for` line name the forms the census reads, and each refused line can be exempted with `# exempt from entry_point: <why>` on its own line above it. An exemption above a block's first statement excuses only the order, so a drifted loop below it is still refused. A new script with a `__main__` block, anywhere in the tree, is now refused until it gives the answer in one of the two forms or carries its reason.
- **Seventeen scripts now give the answer as their `__main__` block's first statement, and five loops that left out `stdin` now name it (#1255).** Twelve made no call at all: under `.github/scripts/`, `close_issues_on_release.py`, `durations_union.py`, `label_merged_on_release_branch.py`, `release_completeness_check.py`, `roll_flow_measurement_issue.py` and `run_tests.py`; under `evals/scripts/`, `noninferiority.py`, `replay_targets.py`, `rule_cases.py` and `seed_defects.py`; and `skills/verify/scripts/mutation_check.py` and `test_layout.py`, which `seal mutate` and `seal layout` run. `close_issues_on_release.py` and `rule_cases.py` put `hooks/lib/` on `sys.path` at import, so they call `console.to_utf8()`, and the other ten spell the loop. Five called `console.to_utf8()` inside `main()`, and now make the call as their block's first statement: `finding_counts.py`, `plugin_directory_check.py`, `publish_release_note.py`, `tag_release.py` and `tracker_labels.py`. The loops of `skills/code-review/scripts/pr_body.py` and of `arm_check.py`, `core_check.py`, `pact_check.py` and `payload_meter.py` under `skills/verify/scripts/` gain the pair `("stdin", "replace")`. Under a cp949, cp932 or cp936 console on Windows, these scripts should no longer die with their stdout empty when they print a character the console cannot encode; that was not run on Windows. `skills/verify/scripts/broad_gate.py` and `seal_stamp.py` ask how stdout was found before the loop moves it to UTF-8, so each carries an exemption above its block's first statement for the order alone. `hooks/lib/launch.py` keeps its exemption: the launcher imports nothing before its floor check. `hooks/dispatch.py`'s exemption, which said its loop was held elsewhere, goes, because the census now reads that loop. `tests/hooks/test_console.py` loses the case that ran `release_report.py`, `release_seal.py` and `rules.py` to read their loops' pairs at run time, because the census reads every loop's pairs.
- **`CONTRIBUTING.md` *House rules* and `hooks/lib/console.py` state the widened rule (#1255).** The bullet **Every file read or written names its encoding.** said every hook entry point opens its `__main__` with `console.to_utf8()` and that the census reads `hooks/`. It now says every entry point, a tracked `.py` with a `__main__` block, opens its first such block with the answer `hooks/lib/console.py#STREAMS` holds, as the call or as a loop over its literal pairs in the one form `tests/censuses.py#loop_pairs` reads, and that the `encoding` and `entry_point` censuses enforce both over every tracked `.py`. The console module's docstring answered its open question, which entry points owe the answer, with every entry point, and says that a script which loads nothing from `hooks/lib/` at import spells the loop, because a load of the module would be one more load that can fail before the answer.
