## Scope

In:

1. **Question 1 holds four answers in the owner's order, `agentic`, `complete`, `manual`, `direct`**, and the exit stays last (G1). Each Description says what its answer does (G2). The text below is what each cell says; its exact words are the build's, under *A label names what it turns off, not where it ends*.
   - **`agentic`**: the orchestrator reads each work item's issue and the tree and chooses that item's route. It chooses which of the framer, `crafter` and `proofer` run, and whether the run may stop to ask. For a change small enough it chooses `direct`. It writes each choice with its grounds, and the person is not asked the route again. Whether the run stops mid-run for a decision only the person can make is the `Automation` row the orchestrator judges, so the cell does not promise that no question ever comes.
   - **`complete`**: the current `automation` cell, unchanged. Every party runs, and the run stops again only for what only the person can decide and would be costly to undo.
   - **`manual`**: the person sets each part, and the four boxes of question 2 are the answer.
   - **`direct`**: this session writes the change itself, with no agent, no frame, no review, no seal and no `routing.md`. Where the root is shared, every commit ends with the trailer `Seal-Routing: no work item`, and the pull request's check only lists those commits. The cell keeps the four nouns `tests/behaviour/test_waiver_decided_at_start.py:465-497` pins and names no destination.
   - **The ` (Recommended)` mark goes to `agentic` every time.** Under that answer the orchestrator reads each item's rung, size and reach itself. A mark taken from the ladder would recommend `complete` for nearly every item here, which is the cost the issue exists to remove (G3). The paragraph *Question 1 is put every time* keeps its first clause, "Question 1 is put every time, whatever the ladder reads", and its sentence on the mark's spelling and on the order. It loses the clause on which rung marks which answer.
   - The `Automation` row keeps its name and its answers `yes` and `no`. The parser reads them (G6). They say whether the run may stop to ask, and `complete` no longer shares their word.
   - The opening paragraph's "two of its three answers settle everything" becomes three of four, `agentic` among them because the orchestrator writes its rows. *The ceiling* says question 1 is at four too, and a fifth answer needs another shape.
2. **A subsection of *Orchestrator: how the work is routed*, `### Under agentic`, holds the guidance for the choice**, as what to weigh and never as a sequence (G18). It states:
   - What the answer is for: time. The orchestrator reads each item and turns off every party the item does not need. Two items of one batch may take different routes.
   - **`direct`** is weighed on four signals, none of them a bound: the change's size; what it touches; whether a gate, a hook or a check script is among it (G29); and how a defect in it would show, whether a suite run at the pull request fails or an agent misreads the text in a later run. A small change whose defect would show at once takes `direct`. A change that touches a gate or a hook takes a work item, and so does one that restates a rule other text states, because finding the class is what a reader does (contract rule 12). The ladder's rung is read beside these signals and does not bound them (G9).
   - **`Planning`**: the framer where the issue leaves a design choice open or the work changes rules several documents state; the session where the issue fixes the change at its coordinate; no frame where the item needs none, which rules out `crafter` (G11).
   - **`Implementation`** by the criterion G17 cites. **`Review`**: the review chain where a reader independent of the build is worth its rounds; `straight to the PR` where the sealer's single reading of the diff against the issue is enough.
   - **`Automation`**: `yes` where every question the orchestrator foresees has a default the tree or the framer can give; `no` where the item reaches outside the repository or waits on a decision only a person can make that the build has to reveal first.
   - **`Destination`**: `open the pull request`, as under `complete`, unless the issue or the person's words in the batch ask for the branch back.
   - **The combinations the invariants rule out**, as a table: a work item headed for a pull request always owes the broad run and the sealer (G10), so only `direct` drops them; `crafter` needs a frame with a plan (G11); a `framer` row needs the framer's mark (G12). Every other combination stands, the owner's framer-alone example (`framer`, `the session`, `straight to the PR`) among them.
   - **Where the choice is written.** For a work item, the rows go into `routing.md`, its line reads `Answered <date> by the orchestrator, before the first edit.`, and *Why this way* names the person who answered question 1 and gives each row's grounds with this subsection as the document judged against. For `direct`, one sentence of grounds stands in the body of the commit that carries the change, above the trailer.
   - **A route is raised when the work outgrows it, and never lowered.** A `direct` change that grows past its grounds becomes a work item before its next commit. A work item routed `straight to the PR` takes the review chain where its diff reaches what its grounds did not name, or where its sealer withholds on that. Each raise is a row of `frame/plan.md` *Judgments of the run* where a plan stands, and otherwise a sentence added under *Why this way*.
   - **What keeps a wrong choice visible**: the pull request's check prints each commit whose trailer reads `no work item` (G13); the commit body holds its grounds; the sealer reads a chosen route against the diff (item 5); the settle reads the release's routes against the fixes that name them (item 6).
