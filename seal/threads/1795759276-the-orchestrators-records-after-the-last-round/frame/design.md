## Scope

In:

1. **The drain commits a row bound for a place outside the work item's directory before the round that reads it.** `skills/implement/orchestration.md` *Orchestrator: drain the rows before the broad run, and keep the records* is rewritten in place, its heading and its table's destinations kept, to state these facts and no others beyond what it holds:
   - A row whose destination stands outside `seal/threads/<work-item-id>/`, a `seal/follow-up.md` row or a policy document written directly, is a commit the chain check gives to a round, as `docs/checks/the-pull-request-check.md` *What the check reads at the pull request* states. The section cites that document and restates none of its rule.
   - The orchestrator commits such a row before it spawns the round that reads it: a leftover a crafter's hand-back names, before the round after that phase or pass, and a finding it defers there, before the verifying round where another finding of that round takes a fix pass.
   - Every other row, one whose destination is inside the work item's directory or is no commit, such as an issue or a comment on one, moves once the last round record has no finding open and before `seal gate --preflight`, as the section says today.
   - A row that first arises once no round is to follow is weighed: its commit outside the work item's directory costs the verifying round the check names, and a home that needs no such commit costs none. The homes are `docs/running/the-run.md` *The run*'s, cited and not restated.
2. **The stop rule agrees.** In `skills/code-review/orchestration.md` *Orchestrator: the run ends with a verifying round*, the paragraph opening **A row that judges the run excessive binds every row after it**, the sentence "That home is rung 2, 3 or 4 of the homes *The run* gives" no longer counts a `seal/follow-up.md` row written after the last round among the homes that cost no round. It cites the drain section of *Scope* item 1 for why.
3. **A case names the path.** In `tests/skills/test_chain_check.py`, under the marker `last-record-at-head`, beside `test_refuses_a_code_commit_after_the_last_record`: a commit that touches `seal/follow-up.md` alone after the last record's `target:` is refused, and the refusal names the commit, the path and `chain_check.py#VERIFYING_ROUND`'s words. The behaviour stands at `e503c6961`, so the case is seen red by mutation alone: `#follows` changed to pass a path named `follow-up.md` directly under the records root.
4. **A judgment row gives no account of what a commit changed.** In `skills/implement/orchestration.md` *Orchestrator: the order inside a ticket*, the paragraph opening **The orchestrator writes its row before it sends a decision** gains these facts:
   - A row of *Judgments of the run* states the judgment, its grounds and the document judged against. It names a commit as a coordinate. It states nothing of what the commit changed: no number of lines, no list of the sentences or items a fold rewrote, no word for the kind of change. `git show <commit>` is that account.
   - The row for a fold states the decision as it is sent. After the fold the orchestrator changes it only where the framer's hand-back names a defect in it or rejects the decision, which the paragraph and the next one already state.

## Rule changes

- add
  file: docs/running/the-run.md
  section: The run
  to: **A judgment row names a commit and gives no account of it.** A row states the judgment, its grounds and the document it stands on. Of a commit it states the name alone. What the commit changed, such as how many lines, which sentences a fold rewrote or what kind of change it was, is read from the commit, and a row that restates it is a second account, which goes false where its writer remembered the commit and did not read it. #1639 weighed a check of the records arm, a `seal` subcommand, a hook and an agent field for these rows and kept them as text: the false rows that release 0.39.2's rounds and its sealer named restated what a fold commit changed or judged what a sentence meant, a script reads neither exactly, and a row that holds no account of a commit has none to get wrong.
